# PONG//EQUIVALENT Palette Lab

Gauntlet 9 changes the workflow rather than betting on one palette at a time.

Instead of choosing a single sound family in advance, the experiment presents ten meaningfully different Web Audio palettes over the same blind-Pong interaction model.

The goal is not to ask which palette sounds prettiest. The goal is to identify which palette is easiest to inhabit as a perceptual language.

## The ten palettes

1. GLASS — clear triangle core with airy sine edge
2. BELL — round sine body with octave shimmer
3. REED — soft square body with a focused nasal character
4. PLUCK — short saw attack with quick decay
5. FLUTE — pure sine with gentle upper support
6. ORGAN — triangle fundamental with fifth support
7. METAL — bright dual partial with slight beating
8. CHIME — high sine ping with a longer clean tail
9. PULSE — compact square click with a strong edge
10. AURORA — triangle body with a close harmonic glow

The physics, paddle control, projected-interception logic, score target, and core audio mappings remain the same inside the lab. The palette changes oscillator family, register, envelope, harmonic support, and spectral character.

## Human test

Play several rallies with each palette. Report the favorite by number/name and, if possible, the reason in perceptual terms:

- easiest to notice without effort
- sounds belong together
- trajectory deformation feels legible
- collisions feel integrated rather than pasted on
- miss sounds wrong in the same language
- least fatiguing
- easiest to remember internally

Do not average the palettes together after selection. A strong winner gives the next build a concrete perceptual foundation to tune.

## Design lesson under test

**Do not tune a sensory grammar indefinitely on top of an unproven instrument family. First find a palette the listener can actually inhabit.**
